How a solo dancer with social anxiety ended up teaching rooms of 70.
View in browser

I realized many of you joined this list through a workshop, a festival or the podcast, and we've never properly met. So here's a little of my story.

By my mid-20s I'd already been dancing for 20 years: hip hop, contemporary, feminine dance styles. Always as a solo dancer.

Then came a breakup, losing my cat and some health issues, and with them, social anxiety. A room full of people felt like too much.

Swing dance brought me back to life. Every Friday night: a sober room full of people, live music, and strangers asking me to dance. I kept showing up.

Then I found contact improvisation and started training at EDAM Dance Company in Vancouver. I knew: this is my thing.

I never planned to teach. I loved the practice so much that I started a podcast interviewing my teachers, and slowly I realized teaching was doable. One day something switched, and I stepped into leading the room.

Today I teach two things: the Momentum Contact Fundamentals workshop, which I've shared 45+ times in 7 years, and the Momentum Contact Facilitator Training, where you learn to teach and share contact improvisation.

If any part of this story feels familiar, I'd love to hear yours. Just hit reply.
